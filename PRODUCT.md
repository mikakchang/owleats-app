# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is a Rice student deciding where to eat in the next meal window. The demo especially needs to serve students who want a fast answer before deciding whether a walk to a servery is worth it.

## Product Purpose

OwlEats turns fragmented Rice Dining information into a personalized daily preview: matched dishes, nearby serveries, special events, and a concise SMS-style summary. Success means a student can decide what to eat with less searching and more confidence.

## Positioning

Unlike a menu directory or social post, OwlEats edits the day down to a student’s saved servery, dietary lens, timing, and the event worth planning around.

## Operating Context

The product is used on a phone or laptop around lunch and dinner decisions. The eventual system will ingest existing dining.rice.edu menu data and deliver opt-in SMS messages through a service such as Twilio. This first demo uses clearly synthetic menu data to validate the experience.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Demo capabilities: saved servery, meal period switching, dietary filters, special-event highlight, SMS preview, and local preference editing.
- Planned capabilities: account creation, menu-data connection, scheduled personalized texts, event calendar, and engagement measurement.
- SMS delivery, real authentication, and live menu ingestion are intentionally not wired into this demo slice.
- Dietary labels must remain explicit and easy to scan.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is OwlEats. The concept is for Rice University Housing & Dining / Hospitality and should feel useful, direct, and student-centered rather than like a generic food app.

## Product Principles

- Make the next meal decision feel lightweight.
- Surface the personally relevant answer before the full directory.
- Treat dietary visibility as a first-class need, not a hidden filter.
- Make special events timely and hard to miss.
- Keep the SMS channel concise, useful, and opt-in.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Dietary accommodations and explicit labels are core to the product. The interface should preserve keyboard focus, readable contrast, and non-color cues for dietary information.
